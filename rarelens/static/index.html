How RareLens was built

    A controlled study

    Every model uses the same data split, network and training recipe. The only thing that changes is where the 500 extra rare-class training images come from — nowhere (baseline), copies, classic augmentation, SMOTE blends or a GAN. Test images are real, never used for any choice, and scored once.

    Limitations, stated plainly

    • 01Small test sets: 20 dermatofibromas and 21 vascular lesions. One image moves recall by about 5 points, so differences between the three augmentation methods are within noise.
    • 02One dataset (HAM10000) and two rare classes. The model only knows 7 diagnoses and always picks one of them — the warning in Examine flags unusual images, but it is not a guarantee.
    • 03GAN images are made at 128 px and enlarged, so they are much softer than real photos. One GAN per disease was trained, so results show classifier variance, not GAN variance.
    • 04Nothing here is clinically validated. Synthetic images are training material, not diagnostic evidence.

    The model inside RareLens

    measured once on the test set

    Four ways to build the app's model were compared: the DF-study GAN classifier alone, or averaged with the VASC-study one, each with or without looking at 8 rotations and mirror images. The one with the best rare-class F1 on the validation set was chosen before its test score was seen. The unusual-image warning measures how far an image is from its 10 nearest training images in the feature space of a general-purpose ImageNet network (deep k-nearest-neighbour check). The classifier's own features are not used for this: they squeeze every picture — even a sunset — into one of the 7 disease groups.
